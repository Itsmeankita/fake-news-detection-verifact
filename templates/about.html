Methodology

How the model actually works.

No black box — here's the full pipeline, from raw text to verdict.

1

Dataset

The model is trained on the ISOT Fake and Real News Dataset — roughly 45,000 labeled articles: ~23,000 fabricated / hyperpartisan articles collected from flagged unreliable sites, and ~21,000 real Reuters wire articles. Each row is just text + label (REAL or FAKE).

2

Text cleaning

Every article passes through the same cleaning function used at prediction time, so training and inference stay consistent:

  • Lowercase everything
  • Strip URLs, HTML tags, punctuation and numbers
  • Remove English stopwords ("the", "is", "at"...) — they carry no signal
  • Lemmatize remaining words ("running" → "run") so related word forms count as the same feature
3

TF-IDF vectorization

Cleaned text is converted into numbers using TF-IDF (Term Frequency–Inverse Document Frequency). It scores each word (and word pair) by how often it appears in an article, discounted by how common it is across all articles — so distinctive, telling words (like sensational phrasing) matter more than generic ones. The vectorizer keeps the top 5,000 unigram + bigram features.

4

Model training & comparison

Three classic, CPU-friendly classifiers are trained on the same TF-IDF features and compared on held-out test data:

Logistic Regression

Learns a weighted combination of word features. Outputs well-calibrated probabilities — used for the confidence score shown on the Detect page.

Multinomial Naive Bayes

A probabilistic baseline that assumes word features are independent. Fast, and a strong classic baseline for text classification.

Passive Aggressive Classifier

An online linear model (similar to a linear SVM) that's especially common in fake-news-detection literature.

The best-performing model on the test set — typically Logistic Regression — is saved and deployed. Exact numbers for this build are on the Analytics page.

5

Evaluation

The dataset is split 80/20 into training and test sets. The model never sees the test set during training, so its accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score reflect performance on genuinely unseen articles.

6

Prediction at request time

When you submit text on the Detect page, the exact same cleaning + TF-IDF steps run, the saved model scores it, and predict_proba() returns a probability for REAL vs FAKE — the higher one becomes the confidence score you see.

Honest limitations

What this model can't do

It reads style, not facts

TF-IDF captures word patterns and phrasing common in real vs. fabricated articles — it doesn't verify claims against real-world facts the way a human fact-checker does.

It reflects its training data

It learns the specific style of the outlets in the ISOT dataset. Well-written misinformation or poorly-written real news can fool it.

It's a signal, not a verdict

Treat the output as a prompt to double-check a claim, not as definitive proof of truth or falsehood.
